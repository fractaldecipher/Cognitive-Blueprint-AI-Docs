### `VEC_OM_AURA_SHIELD_MANDATORY`
- **Phenomenon**: Subconscious Care: Personal Boundary Protection in Crowded Spaces
- **Mentor's Message**: *"Step into crowded places with gentle, clear boundaries. An open heart still requires loving protection against collective exhaustion."*
- **Clinical Interpretation**: Due to a highly sensitive and receptive empathic nature, entering crowded, intense human environments (such as busy airports, high-stress conferences, or bustling commercial centers) without intentional boundaries can lead to sensory overload and nervous exhaustion. Receptive neural circuits absorb ambient tension, requiring conscious self-care and quiet recovery.
- **Actionable Advice**: Anchor a calm, loving sense of your own personal space before stepping into dense environments. Limit prolonged time in crowded areas, and follow high-intensity social events with dedicated quiet time in nature or solitude.
- **System Friction (To Avoid)**: Spending long, uninterrupted hours in crowded, chaotic places without taking quiet breaks; ignoring physical signals of sensory exhaustion.

### `VEC_OM_BLUE_LIGHT_PROHIBITION`
- **Phenomenon**: Evening Sanctuary: Natural Lighting & Protecting Circadian Rest
- **Mentor's Message**: *"Artificial blue light after dark tricks your biology into daytime alert. Dim your lights in the evening to welcome peaceful sleep."*
- **Clinical Interpretation**: Exposure to harsh, cool blue light from digital screens and bright LEDs suppresses natural evening melatonin production. For this sensitive nervous system, late-night screen exposure delays the onset of deep sleep, disrupts restorative slow-wave cycles, and leaves the individual feeling weary upon waking.
- **Actionable Advice**: Turn off bright screens and wear blue-blocking amber lenses after sunset. Replace harsh cool-white bulbs in the bedroom with warm, low-intensity incandescent or amber lamps to support your body's natural sleep hormones.
- **System Friction (To Avoid)**: Working late on bright monitors, scrolling mobile devices in bed, or using intense overhead lighting right before trying to sleep.
